# Reparian_enchroachment_Detector
Traditional government remote-sensing models often rely on coarse or medium-resolution imagery. This leads to severe underestimations of informal structures inside protected riparian zones because clustered homes blend together into a single pixel grid (e.g., detecting only 118 structures when 700 actually exist).
The goal is to accurately isolate and approximate the real-world count of 700 structures within a 60-meter riparian buffer zone.


# Problem Statement
Riparian zones within rapidly urbanizing areas are increasingly affected by informal settlement, land-use change, and encroachment. Accurate identification and monitoring of structures within protected riparian buffers is therefore important for environmental management, urban planning, flood-risk mitigation, and enforcement of riparian protection regulations.
Traditional remote-sensing approaches frequently rely on medium- or coarse-resolution satellite imagery and pixel-based classification techniques. Although these approaches can identify broad land-cover patterns, they may perform poorly in densely populated informal settlements where multiple small structures are located close together. Several buildings may occupy the same pixel or adjacent pixels and consequently be represented as a single land-cover object. This can result in substantial underestimation of the actual number of structures—for example, a model may detect approximately 118 structures where community-level assessments indicate that approximately 700 structures exist.
Manual ground assessments can provide more detailed information, but conducting comprehensive surveys across densely populated riparian areas is time-consuming, expensive, difficult to scale, and potentially unsafe during flooding or other extreme weather events. There is therefore a need for a scalable spatial-analytics approach that combines the accessibility of remote sensing with the object-level detail required for structure detection and counting.
